RevOps Needs a Build Function Now

AI is changing RevOps less by adding another copilot and more by forcing a clearer operating model. The useful split is build vs. run: GTM engineering builds new AI-backed revenue workflows, RevOps governs the system once it touches routing, forecasting, CRM data, and rep behavior.

 

The Org Design Shift: Build vs. Run

The GTM stack is starting to look like an internal product, not a pile of admin-owned tools. That changes the RevOps role.

Landbase frames the split cleanly: GTM engineering builds net-new automated revenue systems, while RevOps governs and optimizes the operating model at scale. That maps to what practitioners are seeing in the market: PM, GTM engineering, and RevOps are being compressed into roles that manage the revenue system with roadmaps, adoption metrics, and change management.

I'd use this split immediately:

1. GTM Engineering Owns Experiments
Build enrichment workflows, AI qualification, account scoring, routing logic, and outbound orchestration. Measure lift, failure rate, and rep adoption.

2. RevOps Owns Controls
Govern field permissions, lead assignment policy, source-of-truth rules, exception handling, and auditability.

3. Sales Leadership Owns Behavior Change
No AI workflow matters if managers do not inspect it, coach to it, and make it part of the operating cadence.

Net: the build function should move fast, but the run function decides what is allowed to become infrastructure.

Use Case: Explainable Forecast Review

Forecasting AI is useful when it explains variance, not when it produces another number nobody trusts.

Tools: CRM opportunity data, activity data, call/email signals, warehouse tables, forecast categories, LLM summaries, Monte Carlo simulation.

Impact: RevOps can move the weekly forecast meeting from opinion trading to evidence review. The output should show commit/best case/pipeline, confidence bands, and the top reasons a deal moved up or down since last week (stage velocity, inactivity, buying committee change, competitor mention, close-date movement).

Build it yourself: Start with a warehouse job that snapshots opportunity state weekly. Add calculated deltas for amount, stage, close date, next step age, meeting count, and executive contact coverage. Then use an LLM to summarize the top three changes per deal in plain English. Keep the model out of final forecast ownership at first. Let it produce the evidence packet, then compare its risk flags against manager judgment for a full quarter.

The tradeoff is governance. Once the model influences commit calls, RevOps needs versioned assumptions and a record of which signals drove the recommendation.

Use Case: Event Signal Engine for Executive Pipeline

Executive events are a good GTM-engineering surface because the signal is rich and the workflow is repeatable.

A practitioner example: online executive roundtables produced $150K to $200K ACV opportunities with no ad spend, no travel, and no third-party event spend. Their reported funnel started with 80 to 160 contacts, 10% to 15% invite acceptance, 75% show rates, and 30% to 35% conversion from attendee to sales call.

Tools: LinkedIn audience building, enrichment, event registration data, transcript capture, CRM campaign membership, LLM topic classification, Slack alerts, sequencer enrollment.

Impact: The AI layer should not write generic follow-up. It should detect executive pain signals from the discussion, classify them by business consequence (forecast credibility, budget approval risk, board pressure, retention exposure), and route only the relevant accounts to sales.

Build it yourself: Treat every event as a signal source. Capture attendee role, company fit, discussion topics, pain statements, and follow-up consent. Use an LLM to summarize each account's pain in one sentence, then require a human-approved reason code before creating the sales task. The goal is selective conversion, not blasting every attendee.

Use Case: AI Routing With a Policy Layer

The next routing system should produce a decision and the reason for the decision.

Tools: Warehouse, enrichment provider, intent source, CRM ownership rules, territory tables, Clay-class workflow builder, Python or orchestration layer, LLM-generated rationale, audit log.

Impact: RevOps can route accounts, tasks, and plays instead of only leads. A high-intent account might trigger enterprise AE routing, partner review, CSM escalation, or a product-led motion depending on segment, current customer status, territory, and recent activity.

Build it yourself: Separate hard rules from AI ranking. Hard rules handle territory, ownership, segment, exclusion lists, and compliance. AI ranks priority and recommends the motion. Write both outputs back to CRM: `routing_decision` and `routing_reason`. That second field matters because reps will ignore black-box routing, and managers need something inspectable when assignments are challenged.

This is where GTM engineering and RevOps should meet: engineering builds the workflow, RevOps owns the policy table, and sales leadership owns the operating cadence.

Use Case: CRM Hygiene Agents With Trust Gates

AI should earn write access to CRM in stages.

Tools: CRM API, enrichment source, validation rules, field-level permission layer, dedupe logic, audit table, human approval queue.

Impact: Low-risk hygiene work can move from quarterly cleanup projects to continuous review. Examples: normalize titles, fill missing industries, flag bounced emails, suggest economic buyers, detect stale next steps, and identify opportunities missing key contacts.

Build it yourself: Classify fields by risk. Let the agent auto-update low-risk fields after validation (job title normalization, email bounce status, industry standardization). Send high-risk fields to approval (owner, segment, stage, ARR, close date). Track precision by field for 30 to 60 days before expanding permissions.

The key is control. AI that touches live CRM data needs allowed operations, audit logs, rollback paths, and a clear owner for policy exceptions.
